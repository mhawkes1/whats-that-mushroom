## What a reviewer is handed

`docs/review-pack.csv` — **629 rows over 128 species, deadliest first**,
generated by `python scripts/review_pack.py --emit`.

Each row is one species and one character: the question as a user sees it,
the answer options the app offers, and the state currently recorded. The
reviewer fills in `AGREE`, `CORRECTED_states` and `REVIEW_notes`.

It is a slice rather than the whole table, and the cut is deliberate.

### Tier 2 — the harmless half of a lethal confusion (84 species, 424 rows)

**Four times the size of tier 1, and leaving it out reviews half a
safeguard.** A state list is read in both directions. Omitting a state a
deadly species shows turns a correct observation into a contradiction and
pushes the lethal candidate *down*. The mirror error on its harmless
lookalike — listing a state it does not show — means the user's answer
fails to contradict the harmless species, so it stays up and the deadly one
stays down. Both halves gate the same warning.

Measured on the real engine: a user who has picked a death cap believing it
to be a field mushroom reports a volva, a white spore print and white gills.
With both halves described, the death cap goes from 30.0% to **96.5%**. With
only the death cap described and the field mushroom left blank, the same
three answers take it from 30.0% to **30.0%** — nothing contradicts the
field mushroom, so the evidence is inert.

Most exposed first: *Agaricus campestris* faces 5 deadly species;
*Calocybe gambosa* and *Marasmius oreades* 3 each; fifteen species 2 each,
including *Amanita vaginata*, *Macrolepiota procera* and
*Volvopluteus gloiocephalus*; the remaining 66 face one.

### Tier 3 — the harmless half of a seriously harmful confusion (23 species, 109 rows)

Same argument, lower ceiling.

### Not in the pack — 143 species, 733 rows

Species with no recorded toxicity and no dangerous lookalike. The app
renders no hazard line for them, so an error cannot change a warning: the
worst case is a wrong name with nothing attached to it. They still want
checking one day. They are not what a first review is for.

## How to complete a row

**How wide a list should be depends on which side of a confusion the species
is on**, and the asymmetry follows from the risk matrix rather than from
taste.

- For a **dangerous** species, list every state it can show, including
  uncommon ones. A state left off turns a correct observation into a
  contradiction and pushes the lethal species down, which is the dangerous
  direction. *Galerina marginata* therefore lists all four ring states,
  because its ring is fragile and often gone, and nobody should be able to
  dismiss a funeral bell for want of one.
- For the **harmless lookalike**, a contradiction pushes the harmless
  species down, which moves the app back toward caution. A tighter list is
  the careful choice here: being wrong costs a false alarm rather than a
  missed poisoning.
- **If you are unsure, leave the row blank.** Blank means "not described",
  which the app treats as no evidence at all, and that is always safe. A
  reviewer who looks at a row and decides it should stay empty has reviewed
  it — *Amanita virosa* does not bruise distinctively, and recording that as
  a blank is the finding.

**A correction must be one of the options listed in the row.** The
`permitted_states` column is a closed vocabulary; anything outside it stores
cleanly and then never matches a user's answer. If an option is missing, say
so in `REVIEW_notes` and it gets added to the catalogue properly — which
also means widening the lists of every species that can show it, because
adding an option contradicts every species that does not list it.

**Three rows in the pack are blank**, including one on *Amanita virosa*
(bruising reaction). An absent character is invisible to the engine rather
than treated as unknown, so those are gaps rather than claims.

## Getting a completed pack back in

```bash
python scripts/review_pack.py --merge <returned.csv>
```

**Do not feed a returned pack to `character_states.py import`.** That tool
is authoritative over the whole table and would silently wipe the 733 rows
the pack does not cover. `--merge` writes back only the covered rows, flags
any correction that is not one of the character's own options, and a test
asserts no uncovered row loses its state.

## Open questions

These are the entries the compiler was least confident about. Each is a
question, not an assertion.

### Nomenclature

1. ***Russula nigricans*** is a synonym of *R. adusta* in both GBIF and
   Species Fungorum, while the British field guides treat the blackening and
   winecork brittlegills as two species. The familiar name is displayed and
   the accepted one recorded alongside, invisibly. Right call? Neither is
   hazardous, so this is data honesty rather than safety — but the image
   fetcher currently files *adusta* photographs under this label.
2. ***Inocybe erubescens*** (DEADLY) is displayed under that name with
   *Inosperma erubescens* recorded as accepted. Nine species carry such a
   pair; `docs/nomenclature-check.csv` lists them all.

### Label space

3. **The ivory funnels.** GBIF treats *Clitocybe dealbata* as a synonym of
   *C. rivulosa* and the label space follows it, so one species stands where
   the field guides have two. If they are separable **in a photograph**,
   re-adding *dealbata* is the change. If they are not, the merge is right
   however the nomenclature settles — a classifier cannot learn a
   distinction a camera cannot record, and no question the app can ask
   resolves it.
4. **The *geophylla* complex.** *Inocybe lilacina* was added and folded back
   into *I. geophylla* on the same day, on the grounds that the white and
   lilac forms want a microscope. Both halves are SERIOUS, so a user saw the
   same hazard line either way. Agreed?
5. **Anything missing.** A species the model has never seen cannot be
   flagged as dangerous: it is forced into the nearest class it knows, which
   may be something harmless. Is there a common UK cause of poisoning that
   is not in the 271?

### Classification

6. ***Paxillus involutus*** is SERIOUS. It has caused fatalities through
   cumulative immune-mediated haemolysis. Should it be DEADLY?
7. ***Tricholoma equestre*** is SERIOUS on the rhabdomyolysis cases. Does
   the dose-dependence change how it should be recorded?
8. ***Amanita rubescens*** and ***Volvopluteus gloiocephalus*** are both
   TOXIC — deliberately conservative rather than a claim about a properly
   cooked specimen. Right, or simply wrong?
9. ***Laetiporus sulphureus*** is TOXIC on host-dependence grounds
   (yew, eucalyptus, conifers). Is host-dependence handled correctly by a
   single flag on the species?
10. ***Omphalotus olearius*** and ***Verpa bohemica*** are both carried. Are
    they genuinely British, or carried over from other references?

### Characters

11. Is any character listed as diagnostic for a species one that does not
    actually separate it in the field?
12. Is anything dangerous missing from a lookalike list? `lookalikes`
    encodes **what people confuse in the field**, not what a classifier
    confuses — the model's own confusions are recorded separately in
    `data/model-confusions.json` and are not a mycological claim.

## Two kinds of sign-off

These are separable and worth recording separately.

1. **Field-character review** — are the recorded states the ones these
   species actually show? This is what the pack asks for, and it unblocks
   the interrogation engine being trustworthy.
2. **Toxicity and nomenclature sign-off** — are the hazard classifications
   and the names right against current literature? This is what blocks
   publication.

A reviewer doing only tier 1 of the first — 96 rows — would still be a
material improvement on where the project stands.

## Where to find a reviewer (UK)

- A local fungus group affiliated to the **British Mycological Society**;
  county fungus recorders are often very experienced
- **University mycology departments**, which are frequently receptive to
  public-good projects
- **The Association of Foragers** — professional teaching foragers
- A **paid consultation**. 629 rows over 128 species is a bounded piece of
  work, and the pack is designed so it can be returned as a spreadsheet
